# Objective Collection

## Objective Collection

Written by CC-3255/01 “Mokka”

## Document Information

- This document contains a list of possible Objectives for our Zeus players and Mission

Makers

- Consequences of Success/Failure can be both short-term (i.e. in the scope of the current mission) or long-term (i.e. in the scope of an overarching campaign)

- The document will be updated irregularly to reflect additions, changes and removals as necessary. We will try to keep you notified as and when changes occur

## Objective Collection

## Forward Operating Base (FOB)

## Description

A fortified base used for staging operations, housing troops, storing supplies, and coordinating attacks.

## Why does it exist?

Main base of operations for the enemy, holds troops and infrastructure for front-line operations.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Enemy loses ability to deploy QRF from this position
- Failure:

- Enemy deploys QRF from this position

## Additional Notes

Can house one or several other (sub-)objectives from this list, unified in the one compound.

## Checkpoints

## Description

Manned positions along roads or strategic points used to monitor and control movement, often equipped with barricades and armed guards.

## Why does it exist?

Control/prevent passage through strategic points. Regulate and monitor traffic, report suspicious movements.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- More freedom of movement for friendly, allied units and civilians
- Enemy control over critical infrastructure (roads) is weakened
- Failure:

- Increased enemy awareness of friendly movements
- Less freedom of movement for friendly, allied units and civilians

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Radar/Communication Stations

## Description

Installations used for surveillance, communication, and tracking enemy movements within the area.

## Why does it exist?

Conduct surveillance and tracking of troop movements. Provide sensor/tracking data on vehicles and aircraft to connected defence batteries. Facilitate communication between installations and to High Command (“orbital command”).

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Delayed responses due to degraded communications
- Less effective vehicle/aircraft tracking
- Failure:

- Better responses do player actions due to high-performance comms
- Increasingly effective aircraft/vehicle tracking

## Additional Notes

Could potentially include an Officer HVT who serves as the “Comms Coordinator”.

## Vehicle Depots

## Description

Vehicle depots serve as storage and maintenance facilities for enemy vehicles, including armoured vehicles, tanks, trucks, and other motorised assets.

## Why does it exist?

Maintain and store vehicles.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Delayed response times for deploying vehicles
- Long-term: Poor vehicle performance due to lack of maintenance leads to degraded performance
- Failure:

- Continuous adequate maintenance and operational availability of vehicles
- Ability to salvage/repair vehicle casualties from prior engagements, replenishing vehicle losses to an extent

## Additional Notes

Would usually be found close to or inside a bigger FOB.

## Supply Depots

## Description

Storage areas for ammunition, weapons, and other essential supplies crucial for the enemy faction's operations.

## Why does it exist?

Logistical hub to supply and sustain operations.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Disrupt enemy logistics, longer response time for QRFs
- Long-term Effect: Decreased combat effectiveness due to impending shortages
- Failure:

- Continuous adequate supply of troops

## Additional Notes

Would usually be found close to or inside a bigger FOB.

## Fuel Depot

## Description

A facility storing fuel for vehicles and aircraft, making it a strategic target for disruption.

## Why does it exist?

Sustain aircraft and vehicle operations.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Limit enemy mobility, hamper vehicle and aerial support due to fuel shortages
- Failure:

- Maintain mobility, easier to conduct vehicle and aerial operations

## Additional Notes

Would usually be found close to or inside a bigger FOB/Airfield/Vehicle Depot

## Artillery/IDF Emplacements

## Description

Fixed positions for mortars, heavy artillery or missile launchers used to provide fire support or bombard enemy positions.

## Why does it exist?

Indirect Fire Support, Bombardments, Area Denial

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Enemy loses ability to call upon Indirect Fire Support
- Failure:

- Enemy continues to use IDF to counter friendly movements

## Additional Notes

Could be connected with a nearby Supply Depot to ensure steady flow of ammunition.

## Training Camps

## Description

Locations where enemy troops are trained, possibly containing shooting ranges, obstacle courses, and barracks.

## Why does it exist?

Educate new recruits and insurgent forces. Train existing personnel to a higher standard.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Slows enemy troop reinforcements
- Lowers overall enemy performance due to halting training efforts
- Failure:

- Enemy is able to replenish lost force more quickly
- Higher overall training standard leads to improved troop performance

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Prison Camps

## Description

Facilities used to detain captured soldiers or civilians, requiring rescue or liberation missions.

## Why does it exist?

Detain and question captured personnel. Use captives as leverage in negotiations.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Possibility to gather relevant intel from liberated detainees
- Deny the enemy access to information and leverage
- Failure:

- Enemy is able to extract sensitive information from captives
- Loss of friendly personnel

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Airfields/Helipads

## Description

Bases for enemy aircraft or helicopters used for transportation, reconnaissance, or airstrikes.

## Why does it exist?

Store, maintain and deploy aerial assets for recon, transport, CAP and attack duties.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Increased response time for enemy air support
- Lack of maintenance on aircraft leads to degraded combat performance in the long term
- Failure:

- Enemy is able to continuously challenge aerial superiority
- Good aircraft maintenance leads to improved combat performance
- Enemy may be able to salvage damaged aircraft and repair them to combat effectiveness, thus partially replenishing losses

## Additional Notes

Would usually be accompanied by fuel and supply depots as well as radar stations and air defence batteries for best performance.

## Intel Centers

## Description

Facilities where enemy intelligence and information are gathered, analysed, and stored.

## Why does it exist?

Gather, analyse and process data in an attempt to extrapolate friendly troop movements.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Reduce enemy awareness of friendly plans
- Potentially succeed in disseminating falsified information in order to confuse the enemy
- Failure:

- Improved enemy ability and predict and respond to friendly operations

## Additional Notes

Could potentially include an Enemy HVT who serves as the leading Intel Officer.

## Weapon/Ammo Manufacturing Facilities

## Description

Sites where weapons and ammunition are produced or modified, potentially containing manufacturing equipment or armouries.

## Why does it exist?

Manufacture, maintain, improve weapons and their ammo.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Reduces enemy access to new weapons
- Lack of weapon service and improvements leads to degraded combat effectiveness
- Decreased combat effectiveness and support availability due to ammo shortages
- Failure:

- Good maintenance and new weapon improvements lead to improved combat effectiveness
- Surplus of ammo leads to more liberal use of supports

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Medical/Droid Repair Facilities

## Description

Hospitals or field clinics where injured enemy soldiers are treated, requiring discretion to avoid civilian casualties. Analogous to Droid Repairs.

## Why does it exist?

Help injured personnel recover to combat effectiveness.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Increases overall enemy casualties as injured personnel can no longer be sufficiently cared for
- Failure:

- Enemy is able to replenish their casualties more quickly as adequate medical care allows them to return to combat effectiveness more readily

## Additional Notes

Interesting moral question whether purely medical installations should be considered as viable military targets.

## Barricades/Blockades

## Description

Physical barriers like roadblocks, walls, or fences erected to control movement or impede progress. Usually not explicitly guarded, unlike Checkpoints. May be trapped to deter attackers.

## Why does it exist?

Restrict/delay access to areas.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- More freedom of movement for friendly, allied units and civilians
- Failure:

- Less freedom of movement for friendly, allied unit and civilians

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Caches/Hidden Stashes

## Description

Secretly stored supplies, weapons, or equipment scattered throughout the area for resupply or emergencies.

## Why does it exist?

Store supplies and equipment in concealed locations, used by insurgents or to facilitate emergency resupplies.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Enemy won’t be able to make use of stashed emergency supplies
- Failure:

- Access to stashed emergency supplies may enable the enemy to recover lost ground when they’re being pushed back

## Additional Notes

Caches may be intentionally placed and trapped as a lure.

## Propaganda Centers

## Description

Buildings or installations used for disseminating enemy propaganda or controlling public perception.

## Why does it exist?

Influence public opinion, mobilise civilian resistance. Disseminate false information to friendly Intelligence.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Reduce enemy’s influence over the local population
- Improves quality and reliability of friendly intel
- Failure:

- Enemy continues to spread misinformation to friendly intelligence, further falsifying reports and degrading the quality of the intel
- Local population may be drawn fully or in part to the enemy’s side

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Minefields/Traps

## Description

Areas seeded with mines or booby traps to impede movement or create hazards for the player.

## Why does it exist?

Impede friendly movements.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Clears path for friendly forces and civilians
- Failure:

- Movement of friendly forces and civilians is further impeded and threatened by hazards

## Additional Notes

Unless driven into a corner, the enemy will not trap/mine their own infrastructure. They might, however, trap an obvious route/point of entry, while they themselves use an alternative passage to circumvent their trap(s).

## Reinforcement Points

## Description

Designated areas where enemy forces are called in for reinforcement during engagements.

## Why does it exist?

Bolster own defences along the obvious avenue of approach. Overwhelm/entrap attackers by guiding them into the reinforcement point.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Denies the enemy the ability to call upon and utilise their reinforcements effectively
- Failure:

- Permit the enemy to continuously reinforce their position and bolster their defences

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Surveillance Drones/Scout Posts

## Description

Locations or airborne drones used for reconnaissance to gather intelligence on the player's movements.

## Why does it exist?

Provide reconnaissance and active guidance for (indirect) fire support systems.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Enemy loses accurate information on friendly troop movements
- Accuracy of (indirect) fire support orders is greatly reduced
- Failure:

- Accuracy of (indirect) fire support order is improved
- Enemy can react more quickly to troop movements and changes to the battlefield

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Siege Emplacements

## Description

Defensive structures set up to guard critical installations or chokepoints, requiring strategic planning to breach. This can be eg. bunkers, trench lines, etc.

## Why does it exist?

Deny or delay access to critical installations. Secured base of fire to deter attackers.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Friendly troops can more easily approach the target location
- Captured Siege Emplacements can be repurposed for own needs
- Failure:

- Secured base of fire can severely damage attacking parties even after pushing past
- Friendly reinforcements will be hindered in their attempt to support the leading force

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Covert Listening Posts

## Description

Small, hidden stations equipped with surveillance equipment, used to eavesdrop on communications or gather intelligence without detection.

## Why does it exist?

Gather information about troop movements in a clandestine fashion.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Disrupts the enemy intel network
- Delayed responses to friendly troop movements
- Failure:

- Enemy can better prepare for incoming attacks

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Chemical/Biological Research Facility

## Description

A secretive installation where the enemy conducts research or production of chemical or biological weapons, requiring careful handling to prevent catastrophe.

## Why does it exist?

Research to be able to produce chemical/biological weapons.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Prevents the enemy deploying chemical/biological weapons
- Securing the research data may allow friendly troops to access the weapons in development for own purposes
- Failure:

- Enemy may be able to deploy chemical/biological weapons to significantly harm friendly forces

## Additional Notes

Could be linked to an Illegal Resource Extraction Site which supplies the necessary research materials.

## Illegal Resource Extraction Sites

## Description

Unauthorised mining or resource collection operations that exploit natural resources for the enemy's gain, leading to environmental concerns or economic disruption.

## Why does it exist?

Secure funds to finance the war machine. Gather materials for chemical/biological weapons research.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Able to transfer funds to friendly forces
- Lack of funding may degrade enemy’s combat performance
- Lack of research material may considerably slow enemy research efforts
- Failure:

- Continued stream of funds can lead to improved combat effectiveness
- Supply of research materials may expedite enemy research efforts

## Additional Notes

N/A

## VIP Safe Houses

## Description

Secured locations where high-ranking enemy officials or informants reside, requiring stealthy infiltration or extraction operations.

## Why does it exist?

Keep HVTs in a secure facility. “Safe haven” to retreat HVTs back to.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Gain access to HVTs and their information
- Disrupt enemy leadership by eliminating key figures
- Failure:

- Enemy’s upper command remains intact and their combat effectiveness is not reduced
- Valuable information is lost

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Air Defense Batteries

## Description

Weapon emplacements situated in the vicinity of sensitive installations, equipped with anti-air guns or missiles to defend against air attacks.

## Why does it exist?

Deny/hinder aerial superiority efforts. Protect sensitive installation against aerial attacks.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Easier access to enemy airspace
- Less friendly aircraft casualties
- Failure:

- Significantly more difficult for friendly forces to attain aerial superiority

## Additional Notes

N/A

## Infrastructure Targets

## Description

Critical infrastructure such as power plants, water treatment facilities, or bridges vital for the enemy faction's control or logistical support.

## Why does it exist?

Essential for enemy’s battlefield logistics and control over civilian population.

## Consequences of Completion/Failure

- Completion:

- Weakens enemy’s control over the local population
- Degrades enemy’s logistics and slows/destroys their supply routes
- Failure:

- Enemy retains strong control over the local population
- Highly effective logistics due to well established infrastructure

## Additional Notes

An interesting point to consider might be how the local civilians would react to their (civilian) infrastructure getting destroyed.
